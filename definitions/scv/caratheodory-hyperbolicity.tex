A complex manifold $X$ is \emph{Carathéodory hyperbolic} if its Carathéodory pseudodistance separates points, namely $c_X(x,y)>0$ whenever $x\ne y$. Equivalently, for any two distinct points of $X$ some holomorphic map $f:X\to\mathbb D$ takes distinct values at them. This is stronger than merely separating points using unbounded scalar holomorphic functions.
