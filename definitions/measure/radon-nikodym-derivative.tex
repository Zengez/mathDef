Let $\mu,\nu$ be $\sigma$-finite positive measures on $(X,\Sigma)$ with $\nu\ll\mu$. The \emph{Radon--Nikodym derivative} $d\nu/d\mu$ is the nonnegative measurable function $f$, unique $\mu$-almost everywhere, satisfying
\[
\nu(E)=\int_E f\,d\mu\qquad(E\in\Sigma).
\]
The Radon--Nikodym theorem guarantees its existence under these hypotheses. Absolute continuity by itself need not guarantee a density without suitable conditions.
