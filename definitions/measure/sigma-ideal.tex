A \emph{$\sigma$-ideal} $\mathcal I$ of measurable subsets of $(X,\Sigma)$ is a nonempty family $\mathcal I\subseteq\Sigma$ closed under taking measurable subsets and countable unions: if $A\in\mathcal I$ and $B\in\Sigma$ with $B\subseteq A$, then $B\in\mathcal I$, and if all $A_n\in\mathcal I$, then $\bigcup_nA_n\in\mathcal I$. It necessarily contains $\varnothing$. The measurable $\mu$-null sets form such an ideal.
