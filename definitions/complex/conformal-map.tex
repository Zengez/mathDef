For plane domains $U,V\subseteq\mathbb C$, a $C^1$ map $f:U\to V$ is \emph{conformal at $a\in U$} in the orientation-preserving sense if its real differential at $a$ is an orientation-preserving similarity with nonzero scale factor. Equivalently, $f$ is complex differentiable at $a$ with $f'(a)\ne0$. A map holomorphic on $U$ and with nowhere-vanishing derivative is conformal on $U$, though it need not be globally injective. Orientation-reversing conformal maps are locally antiholomorphic.
