For a radix-$\beta$ floating-point system with $p$ significant radix-$\beta$ digits, \emph{machine epsilon in the spacing convention} is the gap from $1$ to the next larger representable number, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}=\beta^{1-p}$. The related \emph{unit roundoff} $u$ is a standard uniform bound on relative rounding error for nonzero results in the normalized range when underflow and overflow are excluded. For round-to-nearest arithmetic, $u=\frac12\beta^{1-p}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}/2$, so $|\operatorname{fl}(x)-x|\le u|x|$ under those range assumptions. Other rounding modes give different bounds, and some sources use \emph{machine epsilon} to mean $u$ instead of the gap above $1$.
