Let $\Sigma_0$ be a sub-$\sigma$-algebra of the index-set $\sigma$-algebra $\Sigma$. A \emph{proximity} on the walk space $W(G)$ is a map
\[
P:W(G)\times W(G)\times\Sigma_0\to\mathbb R
\]
such that, for each fixed $A\in\Sigma_0$, $P(w_1,w_2,A)$ depends only on the restricted walks $w_1(A)$ and $w_2(A)$, and satisfies the source's proximity-continuity condition: whenever $d_\tau(w_n(A),u(A))\to0$, one has $P(w_n,u,A)\to0$. This zero-limit requirement is part of the proximity definition; ordinary continuity alone would give only convergence to $P(u,u,A)$.
