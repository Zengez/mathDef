A real random variable $X$ has a \emph{probability density function} $f$ (relative to Lebesgue measure) if $f:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty]$ is measurable and $P(X\in B)=\int_B f(x)\,dx$ for every Borel set $B$. Necessarily $\int_{\mathbb R}f\,dx=1$. Such a density exists precisely when the law of $X$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure and is unique only almost everywhere.
