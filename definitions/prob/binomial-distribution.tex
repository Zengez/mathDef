For an integer $n\ge0$ and $p\in[0,1]$, a variable $X$ has the \emph{binomial distribution} $\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$ if
\[
P(X=k)=\binom nk p^k(1-p)^{n-k},\qquad k=0,\ldots,n,
\]
and $P(X\in\{0,\ldots,n\})=1$, with the conventional endpoint interpretation when $p=0$ or $1$. It is the law of a sum of $n$ independent Bernoulli$(p)$ variables.
