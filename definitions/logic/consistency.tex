A formal theory $T$ in classical logic is \emph{syntactically consistent} if there is no formula $\varphi$ for which both $T\vdash\varphi$ and $T\vdash\neg\varphi$. Equivalently, under the usual explosive classical proof calculus, not every sentence is provable from $T$. Semantic consistency means that the theory has a model; their equivalence for first-order classical logic uses soundness and completeness.
