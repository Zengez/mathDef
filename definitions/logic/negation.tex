In classical propositional logic, the \emph{negation} $\neg P$ of a proposition $P$ has the opposite truth value: it is true exactly when $P$ is false, and false exactly when $P$ is true. The classical law of double negation gives $\neg\neg P\leftrightarrow P$; other logics may use different negation rules.
