A Hausdorff topological space $X$ is \emph{locally compact} in the convention adopted here if every point $x\in X$ has a compact neighborhood $K$, meaning there exists an open $U$ with $x\in U\subseteq K$ and $K$ compact. In Hausdorff spaces this is equivalent to having a neighborhood basis of relatively compact open sets. For spaces without Hausdorffness, competing definitions of local compactness need not be equivalent.
