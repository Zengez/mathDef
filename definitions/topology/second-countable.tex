A topological space $X$ is \emph{second-countable} if its topology admits a countable basis: there is a countable family $\mathcal B$ of open sets such that every open subset of $X$ is a union of members of $\mathcal B$. Second countability implies first countability and separability, but their converses need not hold for arbitrary spaces.
