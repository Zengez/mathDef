A metric space $(X,d)$ is \emph{complete} if every Cauchy sequence $(x_n)$ in $X$ converges to a limit belonging to $X$. Completeness depends on the metric, not only the induced topology: the same topological space can admit both complete and incomplete compatible metrics. Every complete metric space is closed in any ambient metric space in which it is embedded isometrically.
