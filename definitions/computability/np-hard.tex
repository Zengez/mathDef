A decision language $H$ is \emph{NP-hard} under polynomial-time many-one reductions if every language $L\in\mathbf{NP}$ has a polynomial-time computable map $f$ with $x\in L\iff f(x)\in H$. Membership of $H$ in NP is not required. NP-hardness for search or optimization problems needs an explicitly specified appropriate reduction.
