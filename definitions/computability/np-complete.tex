A decision language $H$ is \emph{NP-complete} under polynomial-time many-one reductions if $H\in\mathbf{NP}$ and every language $L\in\mathbf{NP}$ satisfies $L\le_m^p H$. Thus NP-complete languages are both in NP and NP-hard; the definition does not assume $\mathbf P\ne\mathbf{NP}$.
