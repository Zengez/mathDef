A real number $x$ is \emph{computable} if an algorithm, given $n\ge1$, outputs a rational $q_n$ satisfying $|q_n-x|\le2^{-n}$. Equivalently, it can approximate $x$ to any prescribed positive rational precision effectively. A computable sequence of rational numbers that merely converges to $x$, without an effective error bound, need not have a computable limit.
