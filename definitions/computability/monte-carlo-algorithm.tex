A \emph{Monte Carlo randomized algorithm} uses random choices and terminates within a prescribed running-time bound, but its returned answer may be wrong with a specified probability. For example, a bounded-error decision algorithm may have error probability at most $1/3$ on each input. One-sided and two-sided error guarantees differ, and repeated independent runs can sometimes amplify success probability.
