For a continuous map $f:X\to X$ of a compact metric space and $x\in X$, its \emph{$\omega$-limit set} is
\[
\omega_f(x)=\bigcap_{N\ge0}\overline{\{f^n(x):n\ge N\}},
\]
equivalently the set of limits of subsequences $f^{n_k}(x)$ with $n_k\to\infty$. It is nonempty, compact, and invariant in the stronger sense $f(\omega_f(x))=\omega_f(x)$. This uses compactness and continuity, not just forward invariance. Without a compactness hypothesis the set may be empty.
