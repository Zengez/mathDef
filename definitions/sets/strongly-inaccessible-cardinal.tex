An uncountable cardinal $\kappa$ is \emph{strongly inaccessible} if it is regular and a strong limit: $2^\lambda<\kappa$ for every cardinal $\lambda<\kappa$. It is therefore a regular limit cardinal. The weaker notion of an inaccessible cardinal sometimes means an uncountable regular limit cardinal without the strong-limit requirement. If ZFC is consistent, it cannot prove the existence of a strongly inaccessible cardinal.
