### What was consolidated

Confirmed equivalent prerequisites across their exact source contexts include:
- Square-zero ideals with exponent **two** (not general nilpotent ideals); parallel-arrow pairs despite dummy object renaming; the same diagram `F:I→C` used in categorical limits/colimits; underlying indexing and target categories, without inventing smallness.
- Tensor functors `−⊗_R M` (flat/faithfully flat sources), natural comparison `M⊗_R∏Q_i→∏(M⊗_R Q_i)` (Mittag-Leffler sources), the notion of a module product (countable case is an instance of arbitrary indexed case), and finite-rank free modules `R^n` (including the case of multiple ranks in an exact sequence).
- A least upper bound, positive prime integer, sequence of real coefficients, indexed open cover, two-sided identity, clopen subset, multiplication-by-α linear map, normed-vector displacement norm, open neighborhood of a specified point, vector-space dimension and equivalence relation generated by given pairs.
- Scalar extension `A⊗_k K` of commutative unital k-algebras for several **source-specified** extensions K (algebraic closure/perfect closure/general extension). The choice of extension and properties like geometric reducedness/integrality remain source-level hypotheses, not collapsed mathematical theorems.
- An embedded subfield under inherited operations, total order on a set, the Hom-collection under explicit size cautions, general free modules instantiated over localized rings, monic polynomials viewed through a base ring homomorphism, commutative unital coefficient rings and closed continuous maps under various base-change constructions.

### Explicit non-merges: do not redo without new mathematical evidence

These superficially similar pairs were examined and **not** automatically merged:

1. **Radical ideal I** vs. **radical √I of an ideal**. One is the predicate I=√I, the other an operation I↦√I; conflating them loses an essential distinction.
2. **Section** `X→M_i` splitting `M_i→X` vs. the reversed presentation with different cone/projection roles. The review must preserve the actual arrows and composites; equal bag-of-words scores are irrelevant.
3. **R-linear** vs. **S-linear** maps under restriction of scalars. An S-linear map is R-linear, but the converse need not hold, so the typed notions differ.
4. **One pair of functors sharing a codomain** vs. **two functors in opposite directions**. The diagrams impose different typing constraints; generic 'functor' similarity is insufficient.
5. **Coproduct cocone legs** vs. a **common-domain span**. They are opposite-shaped diagrams despite similar descriptions.
6. **Discrete weighted moments by sums** vs. **continuous weighted moments by integrals**. They may both be abstract measure moments but are different source-instantiated constructions without a justified shared generalization.
7. **Covariant** vs. **contravariant natural isomorphisms**: natural isomorphism is an abstract common idea, but the categories of source functors and variance must first be reviewed.
8. **A specifically finite-variable polynomial quotient** vs. arbitrary **quotient ring R/I**: the quotient-ring operation is common, but polynomial generators/relations are extra typed data requiring care before any alias.
9. **Generic field substructures, free modules and finite modules** must not be treated as equalities of *parent definitions* merely because a shared prerequisite was aliased.

These examples preserve object/context distinctions and supply high-value negative examples for future candidate-clustering improvements.
