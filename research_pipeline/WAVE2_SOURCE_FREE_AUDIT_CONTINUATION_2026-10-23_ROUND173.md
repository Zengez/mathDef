## 1. Constructible subsets: previous non-Noetherian warning is a convention distinction, not an incompatibility

Relevant immutable independent source formulations:

- `form.c766a18ce285a2d8.constructible-subset-affine-spectrum`:
  `C⊆Spec(R)` is a finite union of sets `D(f)∩V(g_1,…,g_m)` for arbitrary commutative ring `R`.
- `form.eea0cd4a31af555c.constructible-subset-stacks`:
  A constructible set is a finite union of differences `U\\V` of **retrocompact open** subsets in a topological space `X`.

**Proof they agree for `X=Spec(R)` without assuming Noetherian:**

1. `Spec(R)` is quasi-compact and its distinguished opens `D(f)` form a basis of quasi-compact opens stable under finite intersection. Thus it is spectral. On a spectral space, retrocompact open subsets are exactly quasi-compact open subsets: if `U` is retrocompact, its intersection with `X` (itself a quasi-compact open) is `U`, so `U` is quasi-compact; conversely a quasi-compact open `U` meets any quasi-compact open `W` in a quasi-compact open `U∩W`.
2. Every quasi-compact open `U⊆Spec(R)` is a finite union `⋃_{i=1}^nD(f_i)`. Likewise, another quasi-compact open `V` equals `⋃_{j=1}^mD(g_j)`.
3. Therefore `U\\V = ⋃_{i=1}^n[D(f_i)∩V(g_1,…,g_m)]`, since `Spec(R)\\⋃_jD(g_j)=V((g_1,…,g_m))`. Each retrocompact-open difference has the required affine normal form.
4. Conversely, `D(f)∩V(g_1,…,g_m)=D(f)\\(⋃_{j=1}^mD(g_j))`; both displayed opens are quasi-compact, hence retrocompact. Finite unions preserve constructibility.

**Conclusion:** The two meanings are context-specific presentations of the same notion **on affine spectra**, and the displayed finite equations require no Noetherian hypothesis. Do **not** change either source definition on this basis or equate the general topological definition with all locally closed sets in arbitrary spaces.

**Why the earlier counterexample still matters:** In `R=k[x_1,x_2,…]`, the maximal ideal `m=(x_1,x_2,…)` defines the closed subset `V(m)={m}`, which is locally closed in the ordinary Zariski sense. But `{m}` is not a patch/retrocompact constructible subset: any basic constructible neighborhood `D(f)∩V(g_1,…,g_n)` containing `m` involves only finitely many variable indices, and also contains a smaller prime generated by those finitely many variables. Hence `{m}` is not constructible under either existing independent definition. This refutes the **broader finite-union-of-arbitrary-locally-closed-subsets interpretation**, not the equivalence of the two Wave2 definitions.

Effective graph safeguard: the affine source's final amendment marks the `D(f)` and `V(g_1,…,g_m)` clauses as **missing** primitives (not silently interpreted as every topological closed subset); the retrocompact-topological source separately records a **missing** retrocompact-open prerequisite. Neither formula was changed.

## 3. Submersive maps: surjectivity cannot be inferred from the biconditional alone

Independent source `form.83a528850cdf6a59.submersive-continuous-map` says:

> A continuous map f:X→Y is submersive if it is **surjective** and Y has the quotient topology for f; equivalently, U⊆Y is open exactly when f^{-1}(U) is open in X.

The first clause correctly includes surjectivity. The second clause, read as an *independent* equivalence characterizing all continuous maps, does **not**:

- Take `X={x}` and `Y={x,y}` both discrete, with `f:X→Y` the **non-surjective** inclusion.
- Every subset `U⊆Y` is open, and every inverse image `f^{-1}(U)⊆X` is also open, so the stated open-preimage biconditional holds for every subset `U`.
- Yet `f` is not surjective, so it is not a submersive (quotient) map as defined.

**Scope disposition:** Interpret the biconditional *within* the class of surjective maps; consider explicitly repeating “for such a surjection” in a future authorized source-text improvement, but do not mutate the screened formula in the source-free phase. A non-onto map's final topology on its entire stated codomain need not make it a quotient map under the conventional surjective definition.

**Read-only audit implementation:** `research_pipeline/engine/dependency_global_audit.py` now reports a review-level `submersive_iff_surjectivity_scope` warning when a submersive definition asserts a surjectivity condition and follows it with a potentially scope-ambiguous open-preimage equivalence. The warning explicitly says it is *not* proof that the full source is wrong.
